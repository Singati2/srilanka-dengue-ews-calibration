# MANUSCRIPT_PARAGRAPH.md

As a prospectively locked secondary reanalysis specified after the Version 6 findings were
known but before any alternative-statistics results were computed, we evaluated the
checksum-frozen matched predictions using negative log loss and Brier score. In Sri Lanka,
adding climate improved overall probability accuracy under the matched specification
(recalibrated ΔNLL = −0.0207, conditional 95% cluster-bootstrap interval −0.0353 to −0.0068;
ΔBrier = −0.0079, −0.0130 to −0.0031). In Colombia, the direction was the same but smaller
(recalibrated ΔNLL = −0.0089, −0.0168 to −0.0010; ΔBrier = −0.0043, −0.0078 to −0.0008).
Conditional cluster-bootstrap intervals excluded zero for both proper scores in both
settings (fraction of replicates favoring climate 0.98–1.00, a descriptive sign frequency
that is not a probability of benefit), while development-inclusive intervals were **not
available** because the refit-both-models pipeline was not re-executed in this phase (Phase 1
gate). These threshold-free findings were **concordant in direction** with, but not identical
in emphasis to, the decision-curve analysis: climate improved overall probability accuracy
under the matched specification, but this did not establish improved decisions at the
evaluated alert trade-offs, and no minimum worthwhile increment was elicited. The underlying
matched full-versus-no-climate comparison remained post hoc, and this analysis did not
address complete-case selection, finalized surveillance data, or operational response costs.
