Dear Editors,

We submit our manuscript, **"Benchmarking Climate-Informed Dengue Early Warning Against Recent Surveillance: Calibration and Decision-Curve Evidence from Sri Lanka and Colombia,"** for consideration as a Research Article.

Climate-informed dengue early-warning systems are usually evaluated by discrimination and against weak baselines such as seasonal climatology. We argue, and show, that the operationally relevant question is whether climate adds value beyond information health agencies already hold — recent dengue surveillance — and that this is best judged by calibration and decision-curve net benefit rather than discrimination alone. Using a surveillance-benchmarked, calibration-aware, decision-analytic framework in Sri Lanka (primary) and Colombia (external framework replication with locally refit models), we find that recent surveillance is a strong operational benchmark, climate-only models add limited standalone value, and the hybrid climate–surveillance increment is modest, setting-dependent, and does not increase across 1–12-week horizons.

We believe this fits the journal's scope and its openness to rigorous, cautionary, and replication evidence in neglected tropical disease control. The work makes no claim of climate-only superiority, global validation, deployment readiness, causal climate effects, cost-effectiveness, rare-epidemic prediction, or between-country effect heterogeneity.

The manuscript is original, not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors approve the submission.
