## 1. Journal scope and article types (esp. Research Article)

**Source:** https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/s/journal-information — **fetched: ok**

- Scope (verbatim): *"PLOS Neglected Tropical Diseases publishes research devoted to the pathology, epidemiology, prevention, treatment and control of the neglected tropical diseases (NTDs), as well as relevant public health and policy."*
- NTD definition (verbatim): NTDs are *"poverty-promoting infectious diseases that primarily occur in rural areas and poor urban areas of low-income and middle-income countries."*
- Research Article scope (verbatim): research articles consider *"all aspects of the NTDs, including their pathogenesis, clinical features, pharmacology and treatment, diagnosis, epidemiology, vector biology, and vaccinology and prevention."*
- Article types listed: Research Articles, Editorials, Viewpoints, Policy Platform, Reviews (additional article types are detailed at `/s/other-article-types`).

**Relevance to this manuscript:** A dengue early-warning-system / decision-curve epidemiology study fits the Research Article type (epidemiology, prevention, control, public health/policy).

**Source:** https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/s/submission-guidelines — **fetched: ok**

## 4. Abstract — structured? headings? word limit

**Source:** https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/s/submission-guidelines — **fetched: ok**

- Structured: **Yes.** Verbatim: *"The Abstract is conceptually divided into the following three sections with these headings: Background, Methodology/Principal Findings, and Conclusions/Significance."*
- Word limit (verbatim): *"must not exceed 250–300 words."*
- Other (verbatim): authors *"should mention the techniques used without going into methodological detail and summarize the most important results"*; avoid citations and specialist abbreviations.

## 5. Author Summary — required? audience? word limit

**Source:** https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/s/submission-guidelines — **fetched: ok**

- Required: **Yes.** Verbatim: *"We ask that all authors of research articles include a 150-200 word non-technical summary."*
- Word limit: **150–200 words.**
- Audience (verbatim): *"Ideally aimed to a level of understanding of an undergraduate student."*
- Voice (verbatim): *"write the author summary using the first-person voice"*; *"avoid the use of acronyms and complex scientific terms."*

## 8. Table and figure placement at initial submission

**Source:** https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/s/submission-guidelines — **fetched: ok**

- Figures (verbatim): *"You can include figures in the main manuscript file at initial submission."*

## 10. Data Availability statement requirements and acceptable repositories

**Source:** https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/s/data-availability — **fetched: ok**

- Requirement (verbatim): *"PLOS journals require authors to make all data necessary to replicate their study's findings publicly available without restriction at time of publication."*
- A Data Availability Statement is required in **every** submission (some article types may have no data).
- Minimal data set (verbatim): *"the data required to replicate all study findings reported in the article, as well as related metadata and methods."*
- Repositories: *"appropriate public data repositories"* — subject-specific repositories (examples returned: ArrayExpress, GEO, GenBank, EMBL, DDBJ) and cross-disciplinary generalist repositories; PLOS partners with Dryad and FlowRepository, and uploads Supporting Information to figshare.
- Licensing (verbatim): repository *"policies should not be more restrictive than the Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY) license."*

**Relevance:** Surveillance/climate datasets and any derived analytic data must be deposited in an open repository (e.g., a generalist repository such as Zenodo/Dryad/figshare, or a relevant subject repository) or the DAS must justify access restrictions and explain how to request access.

## 11. Software / code-sharing expectations

**Source:** https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/s/materials-software-and-code-sharing — **fetched: ok**

- Code availability (verbatim): *"We expect all researchers with submissions to PLOS in which author-generated code underpins the findings in the manuscript to make all author-generated code available without restrictions upon publication."*
- Authors should *"Clearly license their code using an open source license"* and *"Share their code via a repository that issues persistent identifiers, such as DOIs."*
- Statement requirement (verbatim): authors must *"State how the code can be accessed in the Data Availability Statement of their manuscript."*
- If code cannot be shared (legal/ethical), document this in the DAS and explain *"how to request access to the code."*
- For software described in the manuscript: license under a suitable open license (e.g., BSD, LGPL, MIT) and deposit in an established open software archive.

**Relevance:** The EWS/decision-curve analysis code (calibration, net-benefit/ΔNB computation) must be deposited with a persistent identifier and an open license, and the DAS must state how to access it.

## 12. Reporting-checklist requirements (STROBE / TRIPOD / others)

**Sources:**
- https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/s/submission-guidelines — **fetched: ok**
- https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/s/best-practices-in-research-reporting — **fetched: ok**

- General rule (verbatim): *"Checklists or other documentation should be uploaded as supporting information at submission."*
- Observational/epidemiological studies (verbatim): *"For reports of epidemiological studies, authors should conform to the STROBE guidelines"*; submission-guidelines wording: *"Observational studies must adhere to the STROBE Statement or alternative guidelines ... and include a completed checklist as supporting information."*
- Diagnostic-accuracy studies (verbatim): *"Reports of studies of diagnostic accuracy should conform to the STARD requirements"* and include a completed checklist as supporting information.
- Clinical trials: CONSORT checklist + flow diagram. Systematic reviews/meta-analyses: PRISMA checklist + flow diagram. Mendelian randomization: STROBE-MR checklist (with page numbers).
- **TRIPOD:** **UNVERIFIED (not found on the fetched PLOS NTD pages).** The fetched best-practices and submission-guidelines pages did **not** explicitly name TRIPOD for prediction-model/prognostic studies. PLOS generally directs authors to the EQUATOR Network for the applicable guideline; for a prediction/early-warning model, TRIPOD is the standard EQUATOR guideline, but this could not be confirmed verbatim from an official PLOS NTD page. Treat TRIPOD as the applicable EQUATOR guideline but verify directly before relying on it.

**Relevance:** A dengue EWS study is primarily an observational/epidemiological prediction study → prepare a **STROBE** checklist as supporting information; consider **TRIPOD** for the predictive-model component (verify on EQUATOR/PLOS).

## 13. Authorship (ICMJE/CRediT), funding, competing interests, ORCID

### 13a. Authorship criteria
**Source:** https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/s/authorship — **fetched: ok**

PLOS NTD uses the ICMJE-aligned (McNutt et al. 2018) criteria. Authors must satisfy all of:
1. (verbatim) *"Substantial contributions to one or more of the following: conception or design of the work; acquisition, analysis, or interpretation of data; creation of new software used in the work; drafting or substantially revising the article."*
2. (verbatim) *"Approved the submitted version (and any substantially modified version that involves the author's contribution to the study)."*
3. (verbatim) *"Agrees to be personally accountable for the author's own contributions AND to ensure that questions related to the accuracy or integrity of any part of the work are appropriately investigated, resolved, and the resolution documented in the literature."*

### 13b. CRediT contributor roles
**Source:** https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/s/authorship — **fetched: ok**
- (verbatim) *"PLOS has adopted the CRediT Taxonomy to describe each author's individual contributions to the work."* 14 roles are listed (Conceptualization, Data Curation, Formal Analysis, Funding Acquisition, Investigation, Methodology, Project Administration, Resources, Software, Supervision, Validation, Visualization, Writing – Original Draft, Writing – Review & Editing).

### 13c. Funding / Financial disclosure
**Source:** https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/s/disclosure-of-funding-sources — **fetched: ok**
- (verbatim) *"For each funding source, provide the name of granting agency, grant number(s), and a description of the funder's role."*
- (verbatim) *"PLOS requires every submission to have a Funding Statement entered into the online submission system before peer review."*
- (verbatim) *"Funding information should be provided at the time of submission through the online submission system."*
- For unfunded research, the standard PLOS statement is: *"The author(s) received no specific funding for this work."* (per PLOS funding guidance; enter in the financial-disclosure field, not the manuscript body).

### 13d. Competing interests
**Source:** https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/s/competing-interests — **fetched: ok**
- (verbatim) *"Declaring all potential competing interests is a requirement at PLOS and is integral to the transparent reporting of research."*
- (verbatim) *"At the time of submission, authors must list all competing interests relevant to the submitted research."*
- Disclosure window: interests *"within 5 years of conducting the research under consideration, or preparing the article for publication."*
- If none, the standard PLOS statement is "The authors have declared that no competing interests exist."

### 13e. ORCID
**Source:** https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/s/submission-guidelines — **fetched: ok**
- (verbatim) *"The corresponding author must provide an ORCID iD at the time of submission by entering it in the user profile in the submission system."*

## 14. Generative-AI / LLM disclosure policy (CRITICAL)

**Source:** https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/s/ethical-publishing-practice — **fetched: ok**

Full verbatim text of the "Artificial Intelligence Tools and Technologies" section:

> "PLOS expects that articles should report the listed authors' own work and ideas. Any contributions made by other sources must be clearly and correctly attributed.
>
> Contributions by artificial intelligence (AI) tools and technologies to a study or to an article's contents must be clearly reported in a dedicated section of the Methods, or in the Acknowledgements section for article types lacking a Methods section. This section should include the name(s) of any tools used, a description of how the authors used the tool(s) and evaluated the validity of the tool's outputs, and a clear statement of which aspects of the study, article contents, data, or supporting files were affected/generated by AI tool usage.
>
> In cases where Large Language Model (LLM) AI tools or technologies contribute to generating text content for a PLOS submission, the article's authors are responsible for ensuring that:
> - the content is accurate and valid,
> - there are no concerns about potential plagiarism,
> - all relevant sources are cited,
> - all statements in the article reporting hypotheses, interpretations, results, conclusions, limitations, and implications of the study represent the authors' own ideas.
>
> The use of AI tools and technologies to fabricate or otherwise misrepresent primary research data is unacceptable.
>
> Noncompliance with any aspect of this policy will be considered misrepresentation of methods, contributions, and/or results. If concerns arise about noncompliance with this policy, PLOS may notify the authors' institution(s) and the journal may reject (pre-publication), retract (post-publication), or publish an editorial notice on the article."

**Where to disclose:** dedicated section of the **Methods** (or **Acknowledgments** if no Methods section), naming the tool(s), how used, and how outputs were validated.

**"AI cannot be an author" — confirmation status:**
- The PLOS NTD **ethical-publishing-practice** page and the **authorship** page (both fetched: ok) do **NOT** contain a verbatim sentence such as "AI tools do not qualify for authorship" or "AI cannot be listed as an author."
- The policy implicitly excludes AI from authorship: authorship requires accountability and approval of the submitted version (Section 13a), which a non-human tool cannot satisfy, and AI contributions are handled via **disclosure**, not authorship. The ethical-publishing page also states (peer-review context, verbatim): *"AI tools cannot serve as reviewers or as decision-issuing editors."*
- **UNVERIFIED (not found):** an explicit, verbatim "AI cannot be an author" statement on an official PLOS NTD page. Do **not** cite such a sentence as a direct PLOS NTD quote until located on an official page; rely instead on the accountability-based authorship criteria + the AI-disclosure requirement above, both of which were verified.
