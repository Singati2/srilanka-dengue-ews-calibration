Climate-informed dengue early-warning systems are usually judged by discrimination (the area under the receiver-operating characteristic curve) and against less demanding comparators such as seasonal climatology. Public-health deployment, however, depends on whether predicted risks improve calibrated, threshold-based alert decisions beyond the information already available from routine surveillance.

We conducted a retrospective decision-evaluation for short-horizon elevated-activity alerting in Sri Lanka (primary, RDHS-week, 2018–2025) and Colombia (a second, differently-constructed case study using a selected common-complete municipality subset, 2020–2022), comparing a recent-case surveillance baseline (M1) with climate-only and hybrid models by calibration, recalibration, and decision-curve net benefit (h=4, reference threshold p^*=0.30). Recent surveillance was a demanding benchmark: climate-only models did not outperform recent surveillance on the evaluated decision metrics, and the design-locked primary hybrid contrast in Sri Lanka did not establish a benefit and was calibration-sensitive: on raw predictions ΔNB(M4-M1) =-0.008 (95% CI -0.028 to +0.013), but because the hybrids (unlike M0–M3) were not recalibrated, an out-of-fold recalibration moved it to +0.009 (95% CI including zero), so neither raw nor recalibrated contrasts established a hybrid benefit. In Colombia the full hybrid exceeded M1 by +0.0188, but M5 adds seasonal terms and department fixed effects M1 lacks; matching that structure reduced the climate increment to +0.0078 (95% CI +0.0039 to +0.0119), about half the compound difference. This small increment did not grow with lead time and attenuated toward zero under a reporting-delay sensitivity (three-week matched +0.0049); its robustness to period could not be established, a development-inclusive refit bootstrap widened its interval to include zero. A post hoc specification-matched decomposition in Sri Lanka (frozen pipeline, validated) gave a climate increment of the same sign in both settings: ΔNB(M5-M5_no-climate) =+0.0087 on raw predictions (95% CI -0.0015 to +0.0188, including zero) and +0.0157 after past-only recalibration of both matched models (95% CI +0.0066 to +0.0257), an interval that excludes zero but is marginal given only 26 spatial clusters and conditional resampling. The point estimate remained positive, but separation from zero was recalibration-dependent, and the magnitudes are of uncertain, setting-varying size rather than established equal. The two settings used differently constructed exposures (humidity in Sri Lanka only) and were not formally compared.

Evaluations that benchmark climate against weak comparators or leave non-climate structure unmatched can overstate the decision value of climate data for dengue alerting. Benchmarked against recent surveillance, matched for structure, and judged by calibrated decision-curve net benefit, climate added little robust standalone value. A structurally matched decomposition gave a small, consistently-signed climate increment in both settings (Sri Lanka +0.0087, Colombia +0.0078) that is not robustly distinguishable from zero on raw predictions and is of uncertain, setting-varying magnitude; in Sri Lanka the matched increment separates from zero only after past-only recalibration (+0.0157, and then only marginally, resting on 26 spatial clusters under conditional resampling), and in Colombia the matched increment includes zero once model-development uncertainty is added—so it is not established, transportable evidence of climate benefit. The contribution is primarily methodological—benchmark against recent surveillance, match non-climate structure before attributing value to climate, and report calibration and decision curves across an operationally justified threshold range rather than discrimination against weak comparators. Local, setting-specific evaluation remains necessary.
